# Investigating-the-SAC-of-3D-Printed-PLA-Discs-with-Varying-Perforation-Geometries
This project investigates the Sound Absorption Coefficient (SAC) of a disc
shaped object made from Polylactic Acid (PLA), a sustainable, biodegradable, and
recyclable polymer widely used in 3D printing. PLA is known for its environmen
tal friendliness, making it a suitable choice for developing acoustic materials. In
this study, 3D-printed PLA discs with various perforation geometries is examined
to understand its sound absorption capabilities, particularly for applications requir
ing lightweight and eco-friendly materials.The experimental investigation involves
sound absorption measurements conducted in an impedance tube, a widely recog
nized tool for acoustic testing. Circular, square, and triangular perforations are cre
ated in the disc, each affecting the material’s acoustic performance in different ways.
The findings have potential applications in sustainable building materials, automotive
sound insulation, and personal audio environments, offering an eco-friendly alterna
tive to conventional sound-absorbing materials.
Among the three tested shapes—triangular, square, and circular—triangular perforations exhibit the highest peak absorption (~85%) around 4000 Hz, making them particularly effective for targeted high-frequency noise control. The square and circular perforations having a value around 70% which can be used for general-purpose acoustic treatments. 
Overall, the results show that triangular perforations are best suited for situations where strong sound absorption is needed at specific high frequencies. On the other hand, square perforations perform more consistently across a wider range of frequencies, making them a better fit for spaces that require broad, uniform noise reduction. In short, choosing the right perforation shape depends on the specific sound control needs of the application.
